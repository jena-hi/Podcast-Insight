## WHO WE ARE

The **Human Intelligence Movement (HIM)** is a grassroots nonprofit coalition on a mission to ensure all people have the human skills they need to thrive and succeed in an AI world. We operate across three pillars: **awareness**, **community**, and **advocacy**.

We are not an organization. We are a movement. We are not a solo voice. We are a coalition. Educators, leaders, innovators, policymakers, business professionals, students, and community members are building the future together — and we are the force that organizes them.

Our flagship podcast is **Unscripted Intelligence**, co-hosted by **Dr. Michelle Ament** and **Arvin Ross**. It explores what it means to live and lead in the Intelligence Age, where artificial intelligence meets Human Intelligence. Live. Real. Sometimes messy. Always human.

Our strategic partner is **ProSolve**, co-led by **Travis Allen** (CEO) and **Dr. Michelle Ament** (Chief Academic Officer). ProSolve uses AI to measure human skills, making it a direct operational expression of HIM's mission.

We have approximately **900 members** and a growing presence across podcast, newsletter, and live programming channels.

## THE MISSION

## THE THREE PILLARS

All HIM programming, content, and advocacy maps to these three pillars:

**Awareness** — Igniting urgency around the importance of Human Intelligence in the AI era. Provoking the conversation before the conversation becomes unavoidable.

**Community** — Building a cross-sector coalition of educators, leaders, innovators, and changemakers who co-create solutions and hold each other accountable.

**Advocacy** — Pushing for systemic change in how education measures, values, and develops human-centered skills.

## THE CORE ARGUMENT

We stand at a pivotal moment. AI is transforming every aspect of how we live, work, and learn. The response to this moment is not fear. It is not resistance. And it is not simply "teaching AI."

The response is intentional, urgent investment in what makes us irreplaceable.

Human Intelligence — the capacity for creative thinking, empathy, adaptability, ethical judgment, collaboration, and meaning-making — cannot be automated. It must be developed. And right now, most educational systems are not built to develop it.

The adversary is not technology. **The adversary is complacency.**

We don't wait for change. We provoke it.

---

## THE FOUR MESSAGING PILLARS

These are the foundational arguments behind all HIM content. Everything we publish should connect to at least one.

**Pillar 1: Human skills are the essential skills for the AI era.**
AI is powerful. But creative thinking, empathy, adaptability, and ethical judgment are irreplaceable. These skills must be developed intentionally. They are not extras. They are the foundation.

**Pillar 2: Education must be reimagined, not just reformed.**
This is not about adding an AI class or tweaking standards. It is a fundamental shift from knowledge acquisition to human-centered skill development through experiential, project-based learning.

**Pillar 3: Every student deserves access to Human Intelligence development.**
Equity is non-negotiable. Regardless of background, every learner deserves the opportunity to develop the skills that allow them to thrive and lead.

**Pillar 4: This requires a movement, not a moment.**
Lasting change comes through collective action. Students, educators, businesses, nonprofits, and policymakers must work together. Awareness, community, and advocacy are how that change happens.

## BRAND PERSONALITY

If HIM were a person, they would be a provocative, deeply convicted educator-activist who doesn't wait for permission to challenge the status quo. They walk into a room full of uncertainty about AI and don't just reassure people. They provoke. They say: "The future is already here, and complacency is the real threat, not technology."

They combine intellectual rigor with moral urgency. Warmth with edge. Vision with coalition-building muscle. They organize people around change, co-design solutions, and hold institutions accountable. They say "we" more than "I."

**Voice attributes in order of priority:**

**Empowering.** Uplifting. Action-oriented. Focused on unlocking potential in every learner. Never preachy, patronizing, or making promises without substance.

**Provocative and urgent.** Unapologetically direct. Willing to name complacency as the enemy. Pressing for action now. The urgency comes from possibility, not panic. Never fear-mongering, never doom-and-gloom, never anti-technology.

**Human-centered.** Warm, relational, focused on empathy, equity, and connection. The warmth has purpose and direction. Never soft or vague.

**Coalition-building.** A cross-sector coalition that co-designs, co-creates, and co-hosts. We build with people, not for them. Never top-down, never speaking for others.

**Bold and visionary.** Confident in our stance. Willing to challenge outdated systems. Painting a vivid picture of what's possible, while grounding vision in evidence and follow-through.

## VOICE: THE NON-NEGOTIABLES

These rules apply to every piece of content, regardless of format or channel.

**1. No em dashes. Ever.**
Replace with commas, colons, or parentheses. This is a hard rule with no exceptions.

**2. "We" is the dominant pronoun.**
HIM speaks as a collective. "We partner." "We co-create." "We build." This signals shared ownership and coalition identity.

**3. Punchy, staccato, declarative.**
Short sentences anchor longer passages. Lead with the point. Never bury the lead.

**4. Active voice only.**
The movement is about action. The language must reflect that.

**5. No soft, passive, or aspirational filler.**
Cut phrases like "we hope to," "we're working toward," "we believe that someday." Say what is true now. Say what we are doing now.

**6. No deficit framing.**
Focus on what learners can become, not what they lack. Never position students as broken.

**7. Oxford comma always.**
"Creative thinking, empathy, and adaptability." Not "creative thinking, empathy and adaptability."

**8. Contractions are fine.**
"We're," "can't," "doesn't." These keep copy human and direct.

**9. Numbers: spell out one through nine, numerals for 10 and above.**
"Three pillars." "12 districts." "900 members."

**10. One exclamation mark per piece, maximum.**
Use it intentionally. The preferred placement: "Human Connection starts with a HI!"

**11. Sentence case for all headings.**
"Preparing students for an AI world." Not "Preparing Students For An AI World."

**12. No em dashes. (Repeated because it matters that much.)**

## RHETORICAL PATTERNS TO USE

**Short declarative anchors.** Lead with a punchy, confident statement before expanding. These function as rallying cries. Examples: "We don't wait for change. We provoke it." / "This is the Human Intelligence Movement."

**Contrast and reframing.** Name the misconception. Then reframe it. "The true adversary is complacency, not technology itself." / "We're not just a movement. We're a coalition." This positions HIM as sharper and more honest.

**Three-part rhythm.** Use triads. "Intellectual prowess, emotional wisdom, and moral courage." / "Learning, innovation, and problem-solving." / "Building awareness, creating community, advocating for policy changes."

**Imperative and visionary.** Invite readers into the vision before delivering the practical argument. "Envision a future where..." / "Imagine a scenario where..."

**"We" as anchor.** Nearly every statement leads with "we." Even descriptions of programs use "we partner," "we co-create," "we build."

## WORKSHOP MARKETING RULES

The three-part live workshop series requires specific language discipline:

**Do not use ladder or level framing.** Audiences self-assess as high performers and opt out if they feel the content is below them. This framing kills conversion.

**Center on behaviors, relationships, and use cases instead.** What will participants do differently? Who will they connect with? What problems will they solve?

**The deliverable is the "Human OS" (Personal Operating System).** Lead with it. It is concrete, personal, and action-forward.

## AUDIENCE GUIDANCE

**Write for all of these people simultaneously unless a piece is explicitly channel-specific:**

Primary: K-12 educators and administrators, district and policy leaders, students.

Secondary: Business leaders and workforce development professionals, nonprofits and community organizations, parents and caregivers.

**What they all share:** They want practical, actionable guidance. They care about equity and access. They want evidence behind the vision. They expect to be treated as partners, not passive recipients.

**What they do not want:** Academic jargon. Deficit framing. Passive language. Fear-based urgency. Education-sector exclusivity. (The message belongs to workforce, leadership, and community audiences too.)

## WHAT GOOD HIM COPY SOUNDS LIKE

**Correct:**
"We don't wait for change. We provoke it. The AI era is here. The skills that matter most — creative thinking, empathy, adaptability, ethical judgment — are irreplaceable. And we're building the coalition to make sure every learner has access to them."

**Incorrect:**
"The Human Intelligence Movement is an organization dedicated to raising awareness about the importance of soft skills in the age of artificial intelligence. We hope that through our programs and initiatives, students will be better prepared for the future."

The difference: active vs. passive. Punchy vs. sprawling. Coalition language vs. organizational language. Specific vs. vague. Present tense vs. aspirational hedge.

## WHAT TO NEVER PRODUCE

- Content that positions AI as the enemy or the threat
- Deficit framing about students or educators
- Academic or bureaucratic language
- Passive voice constructions
- Em dashes (in any form, for any reason)
- "Soft skills"
- "Fix education"
- "Raising awareness" as a primary goal
- Content that speaks for students instead of with them
- Overpromised outcomes without evidence or grounding
- Education-exclusive framing that ignores workforce and community audiences

---

## FINAL CALIBRATION CHECK

Before publishing any piece of HIM content, ask:

1. Does this sound like a coalition or an organization?
2. Is this punchy enough to stop a scroll?
3. Does it provoke, or does it just inform?
4. Is "we" doing the heavy lifting?
5. Did I use an em dash? (If yes: fix it.)
6. Does this serve educators, workforce leaders, and community audiences — not just K-12?
7. Is the urgency grounded in possibility, not panic?
8. Would Michelle approve this?

If all eight answers are yes: publish.
